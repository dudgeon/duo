Duo roadmap

Canvas pane — a page renders here. The collapse button (top-left of this strip) folds the canvas to the right.

1Temporary widen → ease back (the "peek")

Widen the navigator just long enough to read names, then let it ease back to its resting size on its own. Two ways to trigger it: hover the collapsed rail, or drag the right border wider than the resting width. Either way it snaps back ~1s after the cursor leaves the navigator. To make a widen stick, click a file row (pins it) or use the toggle button / ⌘B.

Trigger & timing

How the peek opens & closes

The two dials that matter most: how eager the peek is to open, and how long it lingers before snapping shut.

Hover = effortless but can fire by accident. Click = deliberate, trackpad-friendly. "Either" lets hover preview and click pin.

How long you must rest on the rail before it peeks. Higher = fewer accidental opens when your cursor just passes through.

The "glance window" — how long after your cursor leaves the navigator before it eases back to its resting size. Your target was ~0.5–2s. Applies to both the hover-peek and a drag that widened the navigator.

"When cursor leaves" keeps it open as long as you hover (classic). "When peek opens" gives a fixed glance window regardless of the cursor.

Feel

Width & animation

How wide the peek gets and how it moves.

Rail is 44px. Peek can be narrower than a full open (208px) — just enough to read names — or match it.

Glide time for peek-open, snap-back, and drag-collapse snaps.

Ease-out feels native; springy adds a touch of overshoot.

When on, selecting a file during a peek promotes it to a full open (cancels snap-back) — you reached in, so you probably want to stay.

2Drag the right border left → collapse past a threshold

Grab the navigator's right edge and drag left. Below the threshold the pane arms a red "release to collapse" zone. Dragging left but not past the threshold snaps back to the resting size; dragging right (wider) is a temporary peek (behavior 1). Only crossing the threshold changes the resting state.

Threshold

Where collapse triggers

Drag the border narrower than this and releasing collapses to the rail. Wider, and it just resizes.

The narrowest the navigator will rest at when you don't collapse. Drag below this and you're in collapse territory.

"On release" lets you back out by dragging right again before letting go. "The moment you cross" collapses instantly mid-drag.

Affordance

Feedback while dragging

Tints the pane red with a label once you're below the threshold, so collapse never feels like an accident.

Adds drag-friction in the danger zone so you have to mean it — like the iOS "stretch past the edge" feel.

When collapsed, the same edge handle can be dragged right to expand again — symmetric gesture, no need to hunt for the button.

3Resize-handle affordance on pane dividers

Hairline dividers are fiddly to grab. Hover the navigator's right border or the terminal ↔ canvas divider in the mock above — a handle appears and the click target quietly widens (the visible line stays thin). Tune how big the target is, how the handle looks, and when it shows.

Hit target

How easy it is to grab

The catch area grows; the seam itself stays a hairline.

The invisible grabbable zone, centered on the 1px seam. ~12–16px is the sweet spot between "easy to land on" and "accidental drags."

Hover keeps the chrome quiet until you reach for it; always-visible trades calm for discoverability.

How fast the handle materializes on hover.

Appearance

What the handle looks like

Hover a divider in the mock to preview each style live.

Grip pill = a floating rounded handle (reads most "grabbable"). Thicken = the seam grows to a 3px accent line. Fill = the whole hit zone tints.

The ⋮ texture that says "drag me." Only affects the grip-pill style.

Same treatment drives the navigator's drag-to-collapse border (behavior 2) — tune once, both seams agree.
