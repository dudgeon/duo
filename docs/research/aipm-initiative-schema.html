AIPM initiative graph — schema options

Decision doc Rev 1 2026-07-08 · for the AI-in-Product (AIPM) knowledge base · substrate: OKF vault (brainkit v0.3 / contract v2) · rendered by Duo rollups
What this is. Four candidate data models for tracking AI-adoption initiatives across a federated product org — from a minimal, prose-heavy schema to a fully engineered one — each tested against the six rollups the program actually needs, with representative frontmatter and mock rendered output. Nine decision cards at the bottom lock the shape. How to use it: read top to bottom (~15 min), pick a radio on each card, add notes where you disagree, then hit Copy decisions in the footer and paste the payload back to Claude. Selections persist locally, so you can walk it in more than one sitting.

1 · What we're solving

AIPM is a horizontal program tracking AI adoption across ~2,700 product managers in a federated org. It exists to watch experimentation → see it become declared initiatives → promote what works into a playbook. The data model must support:

  • One durable entity through the progression. A team's declaration ("teams in org_x shall build knowledge bases") fans out into per-team child initiatives; each thread keeps its identity as it matures. Parent/child initiative structure is heavily used.
  • A rich org representation — LOB → org → team chains, leaders, reporting structure — plus the ~60 VPs cataloged (the 2,700 PMs are not bulk-imported; people notes appear when they own something).
  • Two populations per track. AIPM's tracks (Context & Agent Resources, Tools, Training, Tracking & Measurement, …) each need one view uniting initiatives they own and initiatives elsewhere in the business relevant to their domain.
  • Organic use cases, cataloged, themed, tagged to the conceiving org; the mature ones promoted into an external playbook web property (the KB note is the playbook's primary key).
  • Deterministic queries. Rollups do the reading; an agentic pass assigns tags at write time (track relevance, themes) — no query-time fuzzy matching.
  • Teachable to PMs. Project managers will run this. Structured frontmatter only where a rollup filters/groups/joins on it; everything else stays in the markdown body.

Hard limit 1 — a transitive walk follows ONE property name. ancestors("parent") and ancestor:parent:<type> re-read the same key at every hop. A chain that switches names mid-way (initiative.org → organization.parent → …) stops at the switch. Design consequence: whatever edge carries containment must use the same key on every type it passes through. The current KB's uniform parent: is why ancestor:parent:goal works — preserve that.
Hard limit 2 — no query-time joins. A rollup reads each row's own frontmatter (plus ancestor tokens). It cannot filter initiatives by an attribute of a linked note ("rows whose org's leader is a VP"). Join-shaped questions are answered at write time — an agent stamps a derived field on the row — which is exactly the "agentic tagging, deterministic queries" principle already locked. (Nearest-ancestor is also nearest, not topmost: grouping by "the LOB" from deep rows needs either per-LOB views or a stamp.)

Three classes of field (the vocabulary this doc uses)

authoritative a human statement of fact — parent:, owner:, status:. PMs learn these; there are few.
cross-tag relevance links assigned by the agentic filing pass and reviewable — tracks:[], themes:[].
derived stamp machine-maintained denormalization (re-derivable from the graph, brainkit's regenerate discipline) — e.g. exec_org:. Nobody hand-edits these; distill re-derives them.

The frontmatter test stays: if no rollup filters, groups, or joins on it, it goes in the body (timelines, rationale, status narrative — all body).

4 · Autopsy of the current schema

What it already gets right — worth saying plainly, because the recommendation keeps it: a uniform parent: containment spine (own-side chains reach goals; monitor-side chains reach orgs); tracks:[] as an explicit cross-tag rather than query-time matching; engagement: own|monitor as the grouping axis; brainkit-conformant folders derived from the spine. The v0.13.5–6 engine work was built to fit this shape.

The five warts the redesign should fix:

  1. Tracks are type: initiative wearing a costume. Track-ness lives in a "Track: …" naming convention — which is also what detonated BUG-260 (an unquoted Track:  operand parses as a YAML mapping; the filter silently dropped). The engine now quotes defensively, but the type system should carry track-ness, not the display name.
  2. Two theme types (theme — the brainkit starter — and initiative_theme) that mean the same thing.
  3. No maturity axis. The experimental → proven → recommended → required progression (the program's core narrative!) has nowhere to live; status: conflates execution state with maturity.
  4. No playbook fields. Promotion to the playbook is currently only prose.
  5. Exec-level rollups unproven. Q4/Q6 (VP+ views) have no mechanism — the org notes don't say who leads them at what level, and initiatives can't be grouped at "the VP org" granularity by pure chain-walking (hard limits above).

Two enums, one ladder each (per-type statuses: ladders live on the template notes, brainkit-style):

8 · Migration sketch (current KB → Option B)

Every step is a reviewable agent pass (propose-first, distill-style); links heal by id: throughout (duo vault mv / relink), so nothing severs. Rough order:

  1. Pre-flight — duo vault relink --dry-run; mint any missing id: (contract-v2 backfill).
  2. Retype the four track nodes initiative → track; drop the "Track: " name prefix (D9) — inbound tracks:[] links re-point via mv's rewrite; the colon-space YAML hazard class disappears.
  3. Merge themes — initiative_theme notes → theme; retag themes:[] lists to the merged targets.
  4. Add the new axes — maturity: on initiatives + use cases (agent proposes values from status + body evidence, owner reviews); playbook: on use cases.
  5. Enrich orgs — org_level:, leader:, leader_level:, champion: where known; batch-import the VP-org roster (the planned migration source); person stubs for each leader.
  6. Untangle declaration children — where a child's parent: currently points at a spawning initiative but ownership is really an org, move lineage to origin: and re-parent to the org (the only semantic re-pointing in the migration; small population).
  7. Regenerate — templates for track/theme/updated initiative/use_case; duo vault publish; re-render rollups; retire the superseded entity-model-reference.md in favor of the template notes.

9 · Decisions

Decision 1 · of 9

Overall schema shape

The structural choice everything else hangs off. §5's scorecard is the evidence.

Decision 2 · of 9

The parent rule — ownership wins the slot; lineage moves to origin:

Within Option B: when a declaration spawns per-team children, the child's parent: is its owning org/team and the spawning initiative is origin:. One teachable rule ("parent = who owns this work"), and org rollups stay pure chain-walks.

Decision 3 · of 9

Use case: its own type, or a kind of initiative?

A use case is a practice ("draft PRDs from tickets"); an initiative is an effort. Different lifecycle, different destiny (playbook), different fields.

Decision 4 · of 9

The maturity ladder — one shared axis?

Interview locked maturity-as-attribute with values experimental → proven → recommended → required. The current use_case ladder (observed / validated / canonical) says the same thing in different words.

Decision 5 · of 9

Playbook promotion fields

Locked: playbook-readiness is separate from maturity. What shape?

Decision 6 · of 9

Themes — merge to one type, hierarchy deferred-but-free

Today: theme (brainkit starter) and initiative_theme coexist. Themes are finer than tracks; hierarchy may come later.

Decision 7 · of 9

Keep engagement: own | monitor explicit?

Under Option B it's derivable (chain reaches AIPM's tree vs a business org) — but it's also the field the canonical Q1 groups on, and deriving it at query time isn't expressible anyway (hard limit 2).

Decision 8 · of 9

Exec-level rollups (Q4/Q6) — how do VP-granularity views get answered?

Hard limits: nearest-ancestor grouping and no joins mean "group hundreds of initiatives by their VP org" needs help. LOB-granularity versions run natively today via per-LOB ^= views.

Decision 9 · of 9

Track naming — retire the "Track: " prefix?

With type: track carrying track-ness, the display-name prefix is redundant — and its colon-space is the exact string class that produced BUG-260's silent filter drop (now defended in the engine, but why keep tempting it).
