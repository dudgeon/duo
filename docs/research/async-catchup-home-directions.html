Async Catch-Up — four design directions

PrototypeENH-231 Same content model (settled), four genuinely different arrangements · 2026-06-23
What's settled, and what this explores. The nouns and verbs are locked from the prior round — each session reads as a briefing of goal · "You asked" · next-steps · files · artifacts · state, and every card's primary action is either "Open session →" (re-enter: focus-if-live / resume-if-closed) or open an artifact (Review PR · Open report · View diff). This artifact holds that constant and asks the next question: how should the view be arranged? Below are four directions that diverge on real axes — density, time-vs-state organization, prose-vs-structured, and parallel-vs-sequential review — not four skins of one timeline. All four render from the same eight sessions. Pick one (or a hybrid) at the bottom.
Feedback folded in. Sibling Home mode (Projects ↔ Catch-up) · "You asked" labels the human turn, never "last said". One re-entry action — the varied hero verbs collapsed to "Open session →"; only artifact-opening is a distinct primary. Fields are pre-hydrated deterministically (Stop-hook digest); see async-catchup-home.html for the data architecture.
Axes at a glance. ① Inbox = dense / process-fast · ② Briefing = sparse / read-once · ③ Board = spatial / state-first · ④ Deck = sequential / one-at-a-time.

Direction 1 — Triage Inbox

①

The bet: catch-up is queue-processing. Two panes — a dense lane-filtered list on the left, the full briefing on the right — so you sweep top-to-bottom and clear each one. Best for many sessions + a power user. Trade-off: busiest, most "email client", least calm.

Direction 2 — Briefing

②

The bet: catch-up is a read, not a UI. A calm, prose-forward digest grouped by what-you-should-do — like a morning briefing your chief-of-staff left on your desk. Best for a handful of sessions + low cognitive load on re-entry. Trade-off: doesn't scale to 30 sessions; less actionable density.

Duo — Home · Catch-up · Briefing

Direction 3 — Command Board

③

The bet: state is the primary axis. Kanban columns (Needs you · Working · Blocked · Done) turn your delegated work into a pipeline you read at a glance — the height of each column tells you where attention is owed. Best for spatial thinkers + "what's my whole board look like". Trade-off: loses chronology; cards must stay terse.

Duo — Home · Catch-up · Board

Direction 4 — Review Deck

④

The bet: "review the work" taken literally — one session at a time, in priority order (blockers & questions first). A guided pass with Prev / Next / Skip / Mark-reviewed, so nothing gets skipped and each session gets a beat of full attention. Best for deliberate review + small-screen. Trade-off: slowest to get the gestalt; hides the whole-picture.
